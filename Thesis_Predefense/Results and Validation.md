## Static Airfoil at Varying Angle of Attack at Re = 500 

We varied the angle of attack from $0^\circ-30^\circ$ holding Reynolds number constant at 500. The flow is attached and steady at shallow angles of attack. A supercritical Hopf bifurcations occurs approximately at $11.46^\circ$ and a vortex street forms. Vortex are shed alternatingly at an approximately constant strouhal number of $St =$ .  As the angle of attack is further increased a period doubling bifurcations occur around angles $24^\circ-25^\circ$ . We visualized the flow field using vorticity contours. In the obtained wake pattern in the range of angle studied, we identified mode 1, 2 and 3 of Kurtulus(ref).

Time series of aerodynamic loads clearly show demonstrate the three regimes of flow. The pre-shedding cycle has a steady lift value, the post-shedding angles $\alpha>11.5^\circ$ shows an oscillation with a single maximum and the $\alpha > 24^\circ$ show two maxima. This is also apparent in the fourier spectrum.  

The lift curve shows continuous continous rise in $C_L$ value with angle of attack. Notably just after vortex shedding the lift curve shows a sharp change in slope. We might expect that after flow seperation lift should collapse, as it is the case for big aircrafts at high Reynolds number. But in the low Reynolds number case the flow seperation results in a large coherent leading edge vortex, that adds to the total circulation of the airfoil, thereby enhancing lift. In high-Re-turbulent flow that LEV cannot form as it would be immeadiately broken up to smaller vortices.
The drag also sharply increases after the shedding starts, with maximum mean aerodynamic efficiency $C_L/C_D$ value obtained approximately at $\alpha = 15^\circ$
