
### Biot Savart Law for Fluid Mech
A vortex filament is to vorticity what a current-carrying wirse is to magnetism: it induces velocity in the surrounding fluid.
#### General Biot-Savart Law
$$\mathbf{u(x) }= \frac{1}{4\pi}\int\frac{\mathbf{\omega(x)\times (x-x')}}{\mathbf{|x-x'|^3}}d^3x$$

- $\mathbf{x}$ is the point where velocity is to be calculated
- $\mathbf{x'}$ is the source point
#### Vortex Filament
In general by Stokes theorem

$$\int_{A} (\mathbf{\nabla \times u })\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \int_{\partial A} \mathbf{u}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$$

Now make the region in $A$ containing vorticity shrink to a point $P$ while keeping the surface integral on the LHS constant. This makes all the vorticity generate from a point while the rest of the field remains irrotational . The vortex strength of this point vortex is defined by the circulation $$\Gamma= \mathbf{\int u} \cdot dl$$ for any loop enclosing point P. 

This point in 2D is a filament in 3D
- **Point vortex in 2D -> Line vortex in 3D**
- **A very thin tube of vorticity -> vortex filament**

### Helmholtz and Kelvin Theorems

**THREE ASSUMPTIONS**
- **Inviscid**
- **Irrotational** 
- **Barotropic**  : $\rho = \rho (p)$ or $\rho = \text{const}$ 
If these three hold then 
$$\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt} = 0$$
Where $$\Gamma = \int_{c(t)} \mathbf{u}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$$
That is any circulation around any curve surrounding the same material moving at fluid is conserved. This is called **Kelvins Circulation Theorem**
There are a few consequences of this theorem, which are called Helmholtz theorems

1. **Frozen Vortex Filaments**: *vortex filaments move with fluid*
2. **Vortex tube has Constant Strength**: *Circulation strength $\Gamma$ is constant in the entire vortex tube.* If the area of the tube shrinks the vorticity in it increases to keep the surface integral $\int_s \mathbf{\omega} \cdot d\mathbf{S}$ constant . *Same current flows through the entire wire*
3. **Vortex filament cannot end at mid-air**: vortex tubes and filaments either form a close loop or end at infinity
### Lifting-line Theory, wake model
As a consequence of Helmholtz theorem the bound vortex on the wing cannot end in midair, it is bent backwards by 90 degrees, as suggested by Ludwig Prandtl. And it seems to explain the existence of wing-tip vortices. 
This model neglects spanwise vorticity shed into the wake.
It only considers streamwise vorticity being shed into the wake.
