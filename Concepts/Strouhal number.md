$$\text{St} = \frac{\text{convective time }}{\text{unsteady oscillation timescale }}$$
Strouhal number measures the convective time-scale to the time scale of unsteady motion. It asks, *how much has the flow moved in one cycle of unsteadiness* . 

This unsteadiness can be a property of the system, like vortex-shedding frequency of flow past a cylinder; or a forcing function like frequency of pitching and plunging. 

#### Flapping Strouhal VS Chord Strouhal
When an airfoil is plunging at frequency $f$ there are two length scales, the chord length $c$ ; and the amplitude of the motion $A$. The Strouhal number in this case is defined for the plunging motion $$St_{flap} = \frac{fA}{U_\infty}$$
For the other length scale we define a parameter called reduced frequency. $$k = \frac{\pi fc}{2U_\infty}$$
This is actually another Strouhal number, which I call the chord strouhal
Notice that for a given airfoil and airspeed, $k$ depends only on $f$.
